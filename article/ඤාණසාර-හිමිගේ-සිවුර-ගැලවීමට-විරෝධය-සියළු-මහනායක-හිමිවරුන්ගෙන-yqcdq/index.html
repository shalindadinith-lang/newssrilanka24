Senior Monks Urge Caution Over Prison Regulations Affecting Bhikkhus The Trincomalee Monastic Council has expressed significant concern over recent actions taken by the Department of Prisons regarding the presentation of incarcerated Ven. Galagoda Aththe Gnanasara Thero—currently imprisoned under a court ruling—at court in civilian clothing. A special letter has been directed to the General Superintendent of Prisons highlighting these issues. Prominent monks have emphasized the necessity of upholding the integrity of the Constitution and judicial operations in Sri Lanka, asserting that any decision to strip a monk of their robes goes beyond mere administrative or prison regulations. It is considered a sacred duty pertaining to the monastic community, underscoring that the power to make such decisions lies with the respective monastic councils. The council highlighted that any implications suggesting that Ven. Gnanasara Thero is severed from his monastic status creates a situation without any formal resolution from relevant authorities. This action, they warned, poses a significant challenge to the continuity of the Buddhist order and inflicts severe damage on Buddhist traditions. In their letter, the monks referenced Regulation 550 of the Prison Ordinance, calling attention to existing practices whereby prisoners presented in court must wear appropriate attire that reflects their customary dress before being incarcerated. They pointed out that for a bhikkhu, the rightful attire should be the robe worn prior to any allegations against them. This reflects a broader legal principle in line with the United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners, particularly Article 17 (3) of the Nelson Mandela Rules, which stipulates that prisoners should be allowed to wear personal clothing when being transferred outside the prison. Thus, the council has urged prison authorities to ensure that the actions taken do not contravene court decisions or prison laws. They requested the provision of necessary administrative support for Ven. Gnanasara Thero to maintain his dignity and monastic identity during his time in custody and while facing ongoing legal proceedings. The correspondence was jointly signed by Venerable Thibbatuwawe Sri Siddhartha Sumangala of the Malwathu Chapter, Venerable Warakagoda Dhammasiddhi Sri Pannaananda Gnanaratna of the Asgiri Chapter, as well as senior monks from the Amarapura Maha Nikaya and Ramanna Maha Nikaya, and a copy has also been submitted to the Minister of Justice. *Note: In case of any discrepancy between the Sinhala and English versions, the Sinhala version shall prevail.

එය ගරුතර මහා සංඝරත්නය සතු ශාසනික කර්තව්‍යයක් බවත්, භික්ෂුවකගේ භික්ෂුත්වය සම්බන්ධයෙන් තීරණ ගැනීමේ පූර්ණ බලධිකාරය අදාළ නිකායේ හෝ පාර්ශ්වයේ කාරක මහා සංඝ සභාව සතු වන බවත් එම ලිපියෙහි සඳහන් වේ.

ඒ අනුව භික්ෂුවක් සම්බන්ධයෙන් ‘වරදකරු වීමට පෙර ඇඳි ඇඳුම’ වන්නේ චීවරය බවත්, එක්සත් ජාතීන්ගේ සම්මත අවම රීති මාලාවේ (නෙල්සන් මැන්ඩෙලා රීති) 17 (3) වගන්තිය ප්‍රකාරව ද සිරකරුවකු පිටතට රැගෙන යාමේදී පුද්ගලික ඇඳුම් ඇඳීමට අවසර තිබිය යුතු බවත් එමඟින් මතක් කර දී තිබේ.

Senior Monks Urge Caution Over Prison Regulations Affecting Bhikkhus

The Trincomalee Monastic Council has expressed significant concern over recent actions taken by the Department of Prisons regarding the presentation of incarcerated Ven. Galagoda Aththe Gnanasara Thero—currently imprisoned under a court ruling—at court in civilian clothing. A special letter has been directed to the General Superintendent of Prisons highlighting these issues.

Prominent monks have emphasized the necessity of upholding the integrity of the Constitution and judicial operations in Sri Lanka, asserting that any decision to strip a monk of their robes goes beyond mere administrative or prison regulations. It is considered a sacred duty pertaining to the monastic community, underscoring that the power to make such decisions lies with the respective monastic councils.

The council highlighted that any implications suggesting that Ven. Gnanasara Thero is severed from his monastic status creates a situation without any formal resolution from relevant authorities. This action, they warned, poses a significant challenge to the continuity of the Buddhist order and inflicts severe damage on Buddhist traditions.

In their letter, the monks referenced Regulation 550 of the Prison Ordinance, calling attention to existing practices whereby prisoners presented in court must wear appropriate attire that reflects their customary dress before being incarcerated. They pointed out that for a bhikkhu, the rightful attire should be the robe worn prior to any allegations against them.

This reflects a broader legal principle in line with the United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners, particularly Article 17 (3) of the Nelson Mandela Rules, which stipulates that prisoners should be allowed to wear personal clothing when being transferred outside the prison.

Thus, the council has urged prison authorities to ensure that the actions taken do not contravene court decisions or prison laws. They requested the provision of necessary administrative support for Ven. Gnanasara Thero to maintain his dignity and monastic identity during his time in custody and while facing ongoing legal proceedings.

The correspondence was jointly signed by Venerable Thibbatuwawe Sri Siddhartha Sumangala of the Malwathu Chapter, Venerable Warakagoda Dhammasiddhi Sri Pannaananda Gnanaratna of the Asgiri Chapter, as well as senior monks from the Amarapura Maha Nikaya and Ramanna Maha Nikaya, and a copy has also been submitted to the Minister of Justice.
