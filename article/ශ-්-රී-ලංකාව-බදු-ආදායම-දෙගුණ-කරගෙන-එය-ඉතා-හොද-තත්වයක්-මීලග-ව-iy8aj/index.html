Sri Lanka Achieves Notable Economic Stability, Says IMF The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has announced that Sri Lanka is demonstrating significant economic stability and progress in state finances under its comprehensive loan program. With the current program set to conclude in March, the IMF emphasized that the Sri Lankan government will have complete discretion in deciding whether to pursue a new financial program, reiterating its readiness to provide any necessary support. This declaration was made during a special media conference held alongside the IMF's annual meeting for its Asia and Pacific Department, where Deputy Director Sonali Jain-Chandra highlighted the country's current progress. She pointed out that, compared to other nations in the region, Sri Lanka has showcased remarkably strong financial performance. Notably, government tax revenue as a percentage of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) has doubled, and the restructuring process for foreign debt has reached an advanced stage. In response to the severe economic collapse faced by the nation, Sri Lanka has successfully maintained continuous economic growth for 11 consecutive quarters. Jain-Chandra further noted considerable advancements in governance and anti-corruption structural reforms. With several months remaining until the current loan program concludes, two additional review rounds are scheduled to occur. The seventh staff-level agreement is expected to be presented for executive board approval in the coming weeks, followed by the final review process before the program officially ends in March next year. Reflecting on what steps Sri Lanka will take after the completion of its existing loan program, Jain-Chandra indicated that the decision on whether to seek a new financial facility will depend heavily on the government's perspective. The IMF remains committed to supporting Sri Lanka in any capacity, whether through a new loan facility, economic policy advice, or capacity development assistance. In March 2023, following the country's most severe economic collapse in 2022, Sri Lanka entered into an agreement with the IMF for a $2.9 billion extended loan facility. Sri Lanka Achieves Notable Economic Stability, Says IMF

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has announced that Sri Lanka is demonstrating significant economic stability and progress in state finances under its comprehensive loan program. With the current program set to conclude in March, the IMF emphasized that the Sri Lankan government will have complete discretion in deciding whether to pursue a new financial program, reiterating its readiness to provide any necessary support.

This declaration was made during a special media conference held alongside the IMF's annual meeting for its Asia and Pacific Department, where Deputy Director Sonali Jain-Chandra highlighted the country's current progress. She pointed out that, compared to other nations in the region, Sri Lanka has showcased remarkably strong financial performance. Notably, government tax revenue as a percentage of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) has doubled, and the restructuring process for foreign debt has reached an advanced stage.

In response to the severe economic collapse faced by the nation, Sri Lanka has successfully maintained continuous economic growth for 11 consecutive quarters. Jain-Chandra further noted considerable advancements in governance and anti-corruption structural reforms.

With several months remaining until the current loan program concludes, two additional review rounds are scheduled to occur. The seventh staff-level agreement is expected to be presented for executive board approval in the coming weeks, followed by the final review process before the program officially ends in March next year.

Reflecting on what steps Sri Lanka will take after the completion of its existing loan program, Jain-Chandra indicated that the decision on whether to seek a new financial facility will depend heavily on the government's perspective. The IMF remains committed to supporting Sri Lanka in any capacity, whether through a new loan facility, economic policy advice, or capacity development assistance.

In March 2023, following the country's most severe economic collapse in 2022, Sri Lanka entered into an agreement with the IMF for a $2.9 billion extended loan facility.

This marks the 17th IMF program in Sri Lanka’s history, and with the first major foreign debt repayments scheduled to begin in 2028, attention is now turning to whether there will be another technical or financial program following the conclusion of the current initiative next March.
