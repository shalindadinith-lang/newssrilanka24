(NSO) has requested approval from the Public Utilities Commission to raise electricity rates by 2.88% in the upcoming fourth quarter. However, according to the Secretary of the Electricity Consumers’ Association, Sanjeewa Dammika, proper analysis suggests that a reduction in rates between 3% and 5% may be feasible instead. Speaking at a media briefing held in Divulapitiya, Gampaha, Dammika expressed concerns about the proposed increase. He highlighted a contradiction, pointing out that the Minister of Power, Anura Karunatilaka, had previously stated there would be no hike in electricity bills this time. “Despite this assurance, we now find that the NSO is aiming to increase rates by 2.88% in hopes of generating an additional 4.4 billion rupees. Such a move is being justified under claims of financial losses,” he noted. Dammika further explained that the Utilities Commission suggested only a 1.46% increase, translating to an additional revenue of around 2.2 billion rupees. “As electricity consumers, we believe that even this level of increase is unacceptable,” he emphasized. The NSO's proposal cites financial pressure due to the absence of five coal shipments and unsuccessful tenders, stating that consumers should bear this burden. Dammika contested this justification, arguing it is unreasonable for the public to shoulder costs arising from operational setbacks. He reiterated that the government had assured the public would not have to bear the weight of coal shortages. Looking ahead, the Utilities Commission forecasts that hydropower will account for approximately 32.34% of total electricity generation in the next quarter. They further disclosed that the cost of generating this hydropower will constitute about 3.45% of overall production expenses. “This indicates a significant opportunity to utilize cheaper generation methods, which must be passed on to the electricity consumers,” he said. Moreover, the NSO has proposed an additional charge of 669 million rupees for unapproved capacity fees and included 1.069 billion rupees to cover costs for incomplete projects in their rate hike proposal, which Dammika labeled as misguided. In a public notice, the Commission has invited feedback regarding the proposed changes until October 30. Dammika reminded attendees that future hydropower developments could further enhance low-cost electricity generation, encouraging a thorough recalculation of rates based on hydropower capacity, the removal of coal shortage impacts, and exclusion of unapproved payments. He firmly asserted that rates could be reduced by up to 3% if these factors were addressed properly. Calls for Reevaluation as Utility Company Seeks Electricity Rate Increase

The National System Operator (NSO) has requested approval from the Public Utilities Commission to raise electricity rates by 2.88% in the upcoming fourth quarter. However, according to the Secretary of the Electricity Consumers’ Association, Sanjeewa Dammika, proper analysis suggests that a reduction in rates between 3% and 5% may be feasible instead.

Speaking at a media briefing held in Divulapitiya, Gampaha, Dammika expressed concerns about the proposed increase. He highlighted a contradiction, pointing out that the Minister of Power, Anura Karunatilaka, had previously stated there would be no hike in electricity bills this time. “Despite this assurance, we now find that the NSO is aiming to increase rates by 2.88% in hopes of generating an additional 4.4 billion rupees. Such a move is being justified under claims of financial losses,” he noted.

Dammika further explained that the Utilities Commission suggested only a 1.46% increase, translating to an additional revenue of around 2.2 billion rupees. “As electricity consumers, we believe that even this level of increase is unacceptable,” he emphasized.

The NSO's proposal cites financial pressure due to the absence of five coal shipments and unsuccessful tenders, stating that consumers should bear this burden. Dammika contested this justification, arguing it is unreasonable for the public to shoulder costs arising from operational setbacks. He reiterated that the government had assured the public would not have to bear the weight of coal shortages.

Looking ahead, the Utilities Commission forecasts that hydropower will account for approximately 32.34% of total electricity generation in the next quarter. They further disclosed that the cost of generating this hydropower will constitute about 3.45% of overall production expenses. “This indicates a significant opportunity to utilize cheaper generation methods, which must be passed on to the electricity consumers,” he said.

Moreover, the NSO has proposed an additional charge of 669 million rupees for unapproved capacity fees and included 1.069 billion rupees to cover costs for incomplete projects in their rate hike proposal, which Dammika labeled as misguided.

In a public notice, the Commission has invited feedback regarding the proposed changes until October 30. Dammika reminded attendees that future hydropower developments could further enhance low-cost electricity generation, encouraging a thorough recalculation of rates based on hydropower capacity, the removal of coal shortage impacts, and exclusion of unapproved payments. He firmly asserted that rates could be reduced by up to 3% if these factors were addressed properly.

He urged the government to abandon the proposal for an increase in electricity rates, advocating for a sustainable approach, including investments in renewable energy and effective utilization of solar battery systems, which could ultimately reduce electricity costs by as much as 10%.
