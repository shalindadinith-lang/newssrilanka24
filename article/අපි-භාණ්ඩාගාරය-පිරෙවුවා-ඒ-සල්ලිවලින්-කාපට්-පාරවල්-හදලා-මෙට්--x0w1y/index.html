Sri Lanka Enters New Era of Development Funded by Citizens' Earnings, Says Deputy Minister

In a groundbreaking shift, Sri Lanka is witnessing the first instance in its history where development projects are being financed directly by the earnings of its citizens, according to Deputy Minister T.B. Sarath. He emphasized that unlike previous governments, which relied on foreign loans for highway and development initiatives, the current administration is committed to enabling the public to engage in their own development.

Addressing a public gathering, Sarath noted that since the current government assumed power, it has been devoted to transferring all previously stalled development projects into the hands of the people. This commitment underscores a significant change in policy direction, with a focus on community ownership and involvement.

He went on to explain that measures are being taken to gradually replenish the treasury, which was previously in disarray, to secure funding for these projects. Sarath asserted that the ongoing revitalization of the treasury stands not only to benefit the government's plans but also to empower the general public, giving them rightful ownership and respect.

Highlighting the key stakeholders in this success, he remarked that the project’s main beneficiaries are the suffering, innocent, and marginalized citizens of the country. With contributions to infrastructure improvements such as highways, metro systems, and premium bus services, Sarath conveyed that the effective management of the funds generated from the people's earnings is pivotal to these developments.

Furthermore, he reiterated that for the first time in history, a government formed by citizens from their own ranks safeguards the funds they earn through hard work, ensuring that no waste occurs. This transformation, as highlighted by the Deputy Minister, marks a significant milestone in Sri Lanka's approach to governance and development, setting a precedent for future administrations.
