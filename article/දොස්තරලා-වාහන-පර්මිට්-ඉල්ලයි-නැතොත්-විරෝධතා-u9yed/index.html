Government Medical Officers' Association Issues Ultimatum Over Key Demands

The Government Medical Officers' Association (GMOA) has issued a stark warning to authorities, threatening to initiate strong professional actions if five critical demands are not swiftly addressed. Among these are the restoration of the Disturbance, Availability, and Transport (DAT) allowance, reinstatement of suspended additional duties, and the approval of tax-exempt vehicle licenses.

This alert followed an emergency meeting of the association’s central committee, during which a media release was issued under the signature of Secretary Dr. U.K.B. Warakagoda. The association pointed out that in light of the government's claims of economic stabilization, a comprehensive report containing 20 proposals aimed at retaining medical professionals in the country has been submitted to the President and the Minister of Health last July, alongside the upcoming budget.

However, the association expressed frustration, noting that more than three months have passed without any constructive discussions from the authorities regarding these proposals.

During the past two years of economic crisis, the association revealed that doctors have collectively forfeited an amount exceeding 44 billion rupees to the government due to allowance cuts. They emphasized that if this economic injustice continues, it could lead to an exodus of medical professionals, posing a severe risk to the nation’s already strained public healthcare system.

In light of these pressing challenges, the GMOA has laid out five key demands that they urge the government to act on immediately:

  • Full payment of the approved DAT allowances.
  • Immediate reinstatement of the suspended one-third of additional duties and leave allowances.
  • Exemption from PAYE tax on DAT allowances.
  • Expedited integration of eligible doctors under the 22/99 transport facilities scheme.
  • Swift issuance of vehicle import permits with tax benefits for medical practitioners.

The statement warns that should the President fail to intervene effectively in these fair demands, the full authority to decide on potential future professional actions will be delegated to the association’s executive board. They previously ensured that their protests would not disrupt patient care, however, with the government failing to resolve these issues through dialogue, the association emphasizes that all hardships and troubles faced by patients due to their impending industrial actions will rest squarely with the current government, including the President and the Minister of Health.
