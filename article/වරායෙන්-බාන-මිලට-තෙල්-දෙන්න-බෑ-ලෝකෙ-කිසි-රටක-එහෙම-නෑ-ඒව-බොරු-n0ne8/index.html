ඉන්ධන සඳහා අය කෙරෙන බදු අඩු කර ජනතාවට සහන සැලසීම සහ රජය මැදිහත් වී සෘජු සහනාධාර ලබාදීම යන ක්‍රමවේද දෙකෙහිම මූල්‍යමය වශයෙන් කිසිදු වෙනසක් නොමැති බව පෙන්වා දුන් අමාත්‍යවරයා, ක්‍රම දෙකෙන්ම අවසානයේ අහිමි වන ආදායම දැරීමට සිදුවන්නේ මහා භාණ්ඩාගාරයට බව අවධාරණය කළේය.

පසුගිය කාලය පුරා ඔක්ටේන් 92 පෙට්‍රල් සහ ඩීසල් මිල ස්ථාවර මට්ටමක පවත්වාගෙන යාමට හැකිවුවද, පාලනය කළ නොහැකි බාහිර සාධකයක් වූ මැදපෙරදිග යුද ගැටුම් හේතුවෙන් ලෝක වෙළෙඳපොළේ ඉන්ධන මිල සීඝ්‍රයෙන් ඉහළ ගිය බව අමාත්‍යවරයා පෙන්වා දුන්නේය. කෙසේ වෙතත්, පෙර පැවති පාලනයන් මෙන් ඛනිජ තෙල් නීතිගත සංස්ථාව  යළි දැවැන්ත ණය කන්දරාවක සිර නොකර, නිසි මූල්‍ය කළමනාකරණයක් ඔස්සේ භාණ්ඩාගාර ප්‍රතිපාදන මඟින් මෙම අර්බුදය සමනය කරමින් සිටින බව හෙතෙම සඳහන් කළේය.

Sri Lanka Government Allocates Rs. 41 Billion for Diesel Subsidies Amid Rising Fuel Prices

The Sri Lankan government has taken significant measures to safeguard the local economy and consumers by allocating an additional Rs. 41 billion for diesel subsidies over a three-month period from October to December. This announcement was made by Energy Minister Anura Yapa Karunatilake during his participation in the political debate program 'Derana 360'.

Highlighting the strategies employed to alleviate the burden on citizens, the minister emphasized that there would be no financial alteration to the tax reductions imposed on fuel, nor to the direct subsidies provided by the government. He underscored that ultimately, the fiscal burden of any revenue loss associated with these two strategies would rest on the central treasury.

Reflecting on the initial stages of the crisis triggered by soaring oil prices in the international market, Minister Karunatilake recalled the government's decisive action to earmark a staggering Rs. 57 billion specifically for fuel subsidies.

Notably, diesel plays a critical role in the public transportation system, agriculture, and overall industrial production in the country. Therefore, the government’s decision to maintain and extend the diesel subsidy is viewed as essential. Out of the Rs. 41 billion set aside, Rs. 15 billion have already been disbursed for October, while the remaining Rs. 26 billion will support the months of November and December.

Despite successfully stabilizing the prices of Octane 92 petrol and diesel in recent times, the minister pointed out that uncontrollable external factors, particularly the ongoing conflicts in the Middle East, have led to rapid increases in global fuel prices. He assured that unlike prior regimes, the current approach does not confine the petroleum sector within a vast debt crisis, but instead emphasizes prudent fiscal management through treasury allocations to manage the crisis effectively.

Minister Karunatilake also dismissed the notion that fuel supply should be entirely contingent on the cost passed directly to consumers, asserting that such a model is impractical in any market economy. He noted that even historically resource-rich countries like Libya have depended on government support to establish robust subsidy policies.
