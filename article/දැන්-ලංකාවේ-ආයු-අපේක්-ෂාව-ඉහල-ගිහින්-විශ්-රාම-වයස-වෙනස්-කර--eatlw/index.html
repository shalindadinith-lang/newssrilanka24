Justice and Aging: Deputy Minister Calls for Revisions to Retirement Age for Judiciary Experts

With over 1.1 million cases currently pending in the Sri Lankan court system, Deputy Minister of Media, Attorney-at-Law Kaushalya Ariyaratne has emphasized the urgent need to modify retirement age limits to leverage the expertise of seasoned professionals and expedite justice delivery to the public.

Ariyaratne highlighted that the average life expectancy in Sri Lanka has now surpassed 73 years, underscoring the importance of allowing individuals whose health—both physical and mental—remains robust, to continue contributing beyond traditional retirement ages.

Extending the retirement age would enable experienced personnel to provide invaluable insights and services, which would be a disservice to the nation if they were prematurely removed from the system, she pointed out.

She also noted that the speed of releasing government forensic reports, a leading cause of delays in court proceedings, has now been tripled, addressing one of the significant backlogs affecting the legal framework.

The Deputy Minister further elaborated that the government is actively taking steps to enhance both the physical and human resources within legal institutions. Initiatives are underway not only to establish new courts but also to digitize the entire judicial system, ensuring a more efficient operation moving forward.
