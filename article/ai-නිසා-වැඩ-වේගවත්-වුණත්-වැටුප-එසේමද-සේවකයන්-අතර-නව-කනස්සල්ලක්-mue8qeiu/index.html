For many companies, encouraging employees to use AI can be an attractive strategy. Workers who can complete more tasks in less time may help businesses reduce workloads, improve efficiency and increase overall output. But for employees, the situation may be more complicated. ### When higher productivity becomes the new standard Duncan Trevithick, a 34-year-old marketing professional based in Spain who works for an AI training-data company, says he has been thinking carefully about what increased AI productivity means for employees. His workplace offers financial incentives for employees who demonstrate effective use of AI, potentially including bonuses at the end of the year. At first, such incentives may appear to be a straightforward way of rewarding employees for becoming more productive. However, Trevithick questions what happens after workers become capable of completing significantly more work with the help of AI. If AI enables an employee to complete what would normally be around two days of work within a much shorter period, the employee does not necessarily receive two additional days off or a corresponding increase in salary. Instead, the higher level of output could eventually become the new expectation. Trevithick argues that this creates an unusual situation in which employees are encouraged to demonstrate just how much work can be automated or completed more efficiently. ### AI could change the role of employees According to Trevithick, AI could provide short-term career benefits, including opportunities for promotion or greater responsibility. However, he also raises a longer-term concern: by proving that certain tasks can be completed with significantly less human effort, employees may also be providing companies with evidence that some parts of their roles no longer require as much human involvement. This raises a broader question about the future of work. If AI systems can handle repetitive tasks, analyze information, produce content and manage workflows, employees may increasingly be expected to supervise AI rather than perform every task themselves. From managing people to managing AI Trevithick says he is beginning to view his role differently. Rather than thinking only in terms of managing human colleagues, he is also considering how AI agents, automated workflows and other AI systems can become part of his daily responsibilities. “I’m just trying to view myself as a manager, and I’m managing some people, and I’m also managing AI agents, or AI loops, or whatever you want to call it,” he says. The shift reflects a wider transformation taking place across workplaces as companies experiment with AI-powered tools and automated systems. ### The future of AI and workplace rewards The growing use of AI is likely to create new discussions about how employees should be rewarded when technology significantly increases their productivity. Companies may need to consider whether additional productivity should translate into higher salaries, shorter working hours, additional leave, bonuses or new career opportunities. At the same time, employees may need to develop new skills that focus not only on performing tasks but also on managing AI systems and integrating them into existing workflows. AI මඟින් වැඩ කටයුතු වේගවත් කිරීම සමාගම් සඳහා විශාල ප්‍රතිලාභයක් විය හැකිය. කෙසේ වෙතත්, සේවකයෙකුගේ වැඩ කාර්යක්ෂමතාව දෙගුණයක් වුවහොත් එහි ප්‍රතිලාභය සේවකයා සහ සමාගම අතර බෙදී යා යුත්තේ කෙසේද යන්න පිළිබඳ ප්‍රශ්නය තවමත් විවෘතව පවතී. AI භාවිතය නිසා වැඩි ප්‍රතිදානයක් ලබාගන්නා සේවකයන්ට වැටුප්, වැඩ කරන පැය ගණන, නිවාඩු හෝ වෘත්තීය උසස්වීම් වැනි ප්‍රතිලාභ ලබාදීම පිළිබඳව ඉදිරියේදී ආයතනවලට නව ප්‍රතිපත්ති සකස් කිරීමට සිදුවිය හැකිය. එමෙන්ම, AI නිසා රැකියා සම්පූර්ණයෙන්ම අහිමි වේද, නැතහොත් සේවකයන්ගේ භූමිකා වෙනස් වී AI සමඟ එක්ව වැඩ කරන නව ආකාරයක රැකියා පද්ධතියක් බිහි වේද යන්න පිළිබඳ සාකච්ඡාවද තවදුරටත් ඉදිරියට යනු ඇත.
