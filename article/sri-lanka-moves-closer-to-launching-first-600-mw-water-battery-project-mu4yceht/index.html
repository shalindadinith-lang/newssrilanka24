Sri Lanka Moves Closer to Launching First 600 MW “Water Battery” Project

News Sri Lanka 24    2026 බිනර 17

The Ceylon Electricity Board (CEB) has announced significant progress toward launching the Maha Oya Pumped Storage Hydropower Project, which is set to become Sri Lanka’s first large-scale “Water Battery.”

The Ceylon Electricity Board (CEB) has announced significant progress toward launching the Maha Oya Pumped Storage Hydropower Project, which is set to become Sri Lanka’s first large-scale “Water Battery.” According to the CEB, the 600 MW project is designed to store excess electricity generated from renewable sources such as solar and wind. The stored energy can then be used when demand is high or renewable generation is low, helping to improve stability across the national electricity grid. The project, planned across areas of Aranayake and Nawalapitiya, will consist of two reservoirs connected by a 2.5-kilometre tunnel. When surplus renewable electricity is available, water from the lower reservoir will be pumped to the upper reservoir. When electricity is needed, the stored water will flow back down to generate power. The CEB said the project will help Sri Lanka increase its ability to absorb renewable energy while reducing reliance on fossil-fuel-based electricity generation and associated carbon emissions. It is also expected to support the country’s target of generating 70% of its electricity from renewable sources by 2030. The Board is seeking long-term financing from international agencies to help reduce the impact of the project on electricity tariffs and maintain affordable electricity for consumers. The CEB said the Maha Oya project will contribute to Sri Lanka’s energy security while supporting renewable energy integration, creating employment opportunities, encouraging economic development and promoting environmental sustainability.
