Colombo Court Sentences 15 to 220 Years for 2019 Easter Sunday Terror Attacks

A Colombo Special High Court has handed down a staggering 220-year sentence to 15 individuals, including several who were found guilty of colluding with extremist attackers involved in the deadly 2019 Easter Sunday bombings. This brutal series of terrorist attacks claimed the lives of 263 people, including 40 foreign tourists and 35 children.

Additionally, the court has ruled that all movable and immovable properties belonging to these convicts be confiscated. The proceedings included the release of nine other suspects who were acquitted of all charges due to insufficient evidence against them.

The case was presided over by a panel of High Court judges, who concluded that the evidence presented by the prosecution was compelling and devoid of reasonable doubt regarding the culpability of the accused.

According to the charges, the accused were involved in planning and facilitating the bombings at several iconic locations including the St. Anthony's Shrine in Kochchikade, St. Sebastian's Church in Negombo, Zion Church in Batticaloa, and various luxury hotels such as Kingsbury, Shangri-La, Cinnamon Grand, and New Tropical Inn in Dehiwala.

This heinous act not only targeted eight places of worship and communal spaces but also served to incite communal, nationalist, and anti-minority sentiments.
