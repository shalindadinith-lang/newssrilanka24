No Barriers for Private Fuel Companies to Set Prices, Clarifies Chairman of Lanka Mineral Oil Corporation The Chairman of the Lanka Mineral Oil Corporation, D.J. Rajakaruna, has asserted that private fuel companies are free to sell fuel at any price they deem appropriate, with no constraints imposed on them. He further emphasized that unless there is a significant upheaval in the global market, there are no immediate plans for an increase in the prices of fuel sold by Sri Lanka's Sitpetco within the next three months. Addressing the media about the challenges and losses faced by private fuel companies, Rajakaruna pointed out that if these companies suffer losses due to selling fuel at government-mandated prices, there is an existing agreement that the government must compensate for those losses. However, he noted that the government has yet to issue any written directive to these companies requiring them to sell fuel at a fixed price. Currently, these companies are following the price structure set by the Lanka Mineral Oil Corporation voluntarily, which grants them the full freedom to set their prices should they incur any losses, as clarified by the Chairman. He also reassured that regardless of any market fluctuations, the Lanka Mineral Oil Corporation is well-equipped to continue providing services to the public without imposing any additional burden. In a responsible declaration, Rajakaruna confirmed that the corporation will not propose any price increases for fuel until the end of this month or likely for the next three months. එසේම, ලෝක වෙළෙඳපොළේ දැවැන්ත පෙරළියක් සිදු නොවන්නේ නම්, එළැඹෙන මාස තුනක කාලය තුළ සිපෙට්කෝ ඉන්ධන මිල ඉහළ දැමීමේ සූදානමක් නොමැති බව ද හෙතෙම අවධාරණය කළේය.

පෞද්ගලික ඉන්ධන සමාගම් මුහුණ දී ඇති බව කියන පාඩුව සහ අර්බුදය සම්බන්ධයෙන් මාධ්‍ය වෙත අදහස් දක්වමින් සභාපතිවරයා පෙන්වා දුන්නේ, යම් හෙයකින් රජය නියම කරන මිලකට ඉන්ධන අලෙවි කිරීමට සිදුවීමෙන් එම සමාගම්වලට අලාභයක් සිදුවුවහොත්, එම පාඩුව රජය විසින් පියවිය යුතු බවට ගිවිසුම්ගත කොන්දේසියක් පවතින බවයි.

කෙසේ වෙතත්, කිසියම් නිශ්චිත මිලකට ඉන්ධන අලෙවි කරන ලෙස රජය මෙතෙක් එම සමාගම් වෙත ලිඛිතව කිසිදු නියෝගයක් නිකුත් කර නොමැති බව ඔහු සඳහන් කළේය.

එම සමාගම් දැනට සිදු කරන්නේ ඛනිජ තෙල් නීතිගත සංස්ථාවේ මිල ගණන් ස්වේච්ඡාවෙන්ම අනුගමනය කිරීම පමණක් බවත්, එබැවින් පාඩු ලබන්නේ නම් තමන්ට කැමති මිලක් නියම කර ඉන්ධන අලෙවි කිරීමට එම සමාගම්වලට පූර්ණ නිදහස පවතින බවත් සභාපතිවරයා පැහැදිලි කළේය.

මේ අතර, වෙළෙඳපොළේ කුමන අර්බුදයක් නිර්මාණය වුව ද ඒ සියල්ලට මුහුණ දී ජනතාවට බරක් නොවී සේවය සැපයීමේ හැකියාව ලංකා ඛනිජ තෙල් නීතිගත සංස්ථාව සතුව පවතින බව ඔහු කියා සිටියේය.

No Barriers for Private Fuel Companies to Set Prices, Clarifies Chairman of Lanka Mineral Oil Corporation

The Chairman of the Lanka Mineral Oil Corporation, D.J. Rajakaruna, has asserted that private fuel companies are free to sell fuel at any price they deem appropriate, with no constraints imposed on them.

He further emphasized that unless there is a significant upheaval in the global market, there are no immediate plans for an increase in the prices of fuel sold by Sri Lanka's Sitpetco within the next three months.

Addressing the media about the challenges and losses faced by private fuel companies, Rajakaruna pointed out that if these companies suffer losses due to selling fuel at government-mandated prices, there is an existing agreement that the government must compensate for those losses.

However, he noted that the government has yet to issue any written directive to these companies requiring them to sell fuel at a fixed price.

Currently, these companies are following the price structure set by the Lanka Mineral Oil Corporation voluntarily, which grants them the full freedom to set their prices should they incur any losses, as clarified by the Chairman.

He also reassured that regardless of any market fluctuations, the Lanka Mineral Oil Corporation is well-equipped to continue providing services to the public without imposing any additional burden.

In a responsible declaration, Rajakaruna confirmed that the corporation will not propose any price increases for fuel until the end of this month or likely for the next three months.

Additionally, he urged the public not to harbor undue anxiety regarding the country’s fuel reserves, assuring that steps are being actively taken to manage the reserves effectively without any issues going forward.
